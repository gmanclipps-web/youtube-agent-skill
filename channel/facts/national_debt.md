# National debt

- The national debt is the amount of money the federal government has borrowed to cover expenses over time; a deficit happens in a year when spending exceeds revenue. (source: https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/americas-finance-guide/national-debt/)
- The government borrows by selling Treasury securities such as bills, notes and bonds; the debt is the accumulation of this borrowing plus interest owed. (source: https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/americas-finance-guide/national-debt/)
- Debts from the Revolutionary War amounted to over $75 million by January 1, 1791. (source: https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/americas-finance-guide/national-debt/)
- The debt was briefly eliminated in 1835 thanks to sales of federal land and budget cuts. (source: https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/americas-finance-guide/national-debt/)
- During the Civil War the debt grew from $65 million in 1860 to $1 billion in 1863. (source: https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/americas-finance-guide/national-debt/)
- The U.S. debt to GDP ratio passed 100 percent in 2013, when debt and GDP were both about 16.7 trillion dollars. (source: https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/americas-finance-guide/national-debt/)
