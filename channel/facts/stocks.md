# Stocks

- A stock is a security that gives stockholders a share of ownership in a company; stocks are also called equities. (source: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/stocks)
- Companies issue stock to raise money, for example to pay off debt, launch new products or build new facilities. (source: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/stocks)
- There are two main kinds of stock: common stock and preferred stock. (source: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/stocks)
- Investors who stuck with stocks over long periods, say 15 years, generally have been rewarded, but stock prices move down as well as up and you can lose money. (source: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/stocks)
- Large company stocks as a group have lost money on average about 1 out of every 3 years. (source: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/stocks)
- If a company goes bankrupt, bondholders are paid first, then preferred stockholders; common stockholders are last and may get nothing. (source: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/stocks)
