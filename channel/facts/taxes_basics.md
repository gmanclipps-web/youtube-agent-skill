# Tax basics

- You pay federal income tax in layers called brackets: when income moves into a higher bracket, only the part in that bracket is taxed at the higher rate. (source: https://www.irs.gov/filing/federal-income-tax-rates-and-brackets)
- There are 7 federal income tax rates: 10, 12, 22, 24, 32, 35 and 37 percent. (source: https://www.irs.gov/filing/federal-income-tax-rates-and-brackets)
- For tax year 2026 the standard deduction is $16,100 for single filers and $32,200 for married couples filing jointly. (source: https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-releases-tax-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-year-2026-including-amendments-from-the-one-big-beautiful-bill)
- For tax year 2026 the 37 percent top rate applies to single filers with income over $640,600. (source: https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-releases-tax-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-year-2026-including-amendments-from-the-one-big-beautiful-bill)
- The filing deadline for most people (calendar year filers) was April 15, 2026. (source: https://www.irs.gov/filing/individuals/when-to-file)
- You can request an automatic 6-month extension to file, but an extension to file is not an extension to pay. (source: https://www.irs.gov/filing/individuals/when-to-file)
