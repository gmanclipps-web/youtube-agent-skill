# Printing money

- The Treasury's Bureau of Engraving and Printing has made the nation's paper currency since 1862, printing it in Washington, D.C. and Fort Worth, Texas. (source: https://www.bep.gov/currency/how-money-is-made)
- Each year the Federal Reserve Board projects demand for new currency and places an order with the Bureau of Engraving and Printing. (source: https://www.federalreserve.gov/faqs/currency_12771.htm)
- U.S. currency paper is 75 percent cotton and 25 percent linen, with red and blue fibers mixed in. (source: https://www.bep.gov/currency/how-money-is-made)
- In the 2025 budget, the variable printing cost was 4.1 cents for a $1 note and 11.3 cents for a $100 note. (source: https://www.federalreserve.gov/faqs/currency_12771.htm)
- The value of U.S. currency in circulation was $2,394.9 billion at the end of 2025, up from $758.8 billion at the end of 2005. (source: https://www.federalreserve.gov/paymentsystems/coin_currcircvalue.htm)
- The money supply is more than cash: it includes coins and balances in checking and savings accounts. (source: https://www.federalreserve.gov/faqs/money_12845.htm)
