# Tariffs

- A tariff or duty (the words are used interchangeably) is a tax levied by governments on the value of imported products, including freight and insurance. (source: https://www.trade.gov/import-tariffs-fees-overview-and-resources)
- The tariff is collected at the time of customs clearance, and tariffs and taxes increase the cost of a product to the buyer. (source: https://www.trade.gov/import-tariffs-fees-overview-and-resources)
- In the U.S., the importer is ultimately responsible for paying any duty owed on an import, not the foreign seller. (source: https://www.cbp.gov/trade/basic-import-export/internet-purchases)
- The Harmonized Tariff Schedule gives duty rates for virtually every item and is the size of an unabridged dictionary. (source: https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/determining-duty-rates)
- U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP), not the importer, makes the final determination of the correct rate of duty. (source: https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/determining-duty-rates)
- The U.S. has free trade agreements with more than 20 countries, which can let goods enter duty-free. (source: https://www.trade.gov/import-tariffs-fees-overview-and-resources)
