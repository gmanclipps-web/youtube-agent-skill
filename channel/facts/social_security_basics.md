# Social Security

- Workers earn Social Security credits by working and paying Social Security taxes, up to 4 credits a year, and need 40 credits to qualify for retirement benefits. (source: https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/credits.html)
- Retirement benefits can start as early as age 62, but full benefits begin only at full retirement age, which is 67 for people born in 1960 or later. (source: https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/agereduction.html)
- SSA example: for someone born in 1960 or later, a $1,000 monthly benefit at full retirement age shrinks to $700 if started at 62, a 30 percent cut. (source: https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/agereduction.html)
- Waiting past full retirement age raises the benefit by 8 percent for each year of delay (for people born in 1943 or later), and the increase stops at age 70. (source: https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/delayret.html)
- Social Security and SSI benefits for 76 million Americans will rise 3.6 percent in 2027, starting with January 2027 payments for nearly 72 million Social Security beneficiaries. (as of: 2026-10-14) (source: https://www.ssa.gov/cola/)
- In 2027 the maximum amount of earnings subject to the Social Security tax rises to $192,300. (as of: 2026-10-14) (source: https://www.ssa.gov/cola/)
- Automatic yearly cost-of-living raises began in 1975; the largest was 14.3 percent in July 1980, and there was no raise at all in 2010, 2011 and 2016. (source: https://www.ssa.gov/cola/)
