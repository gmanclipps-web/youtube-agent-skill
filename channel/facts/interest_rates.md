# Interest rates

- The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) sets U.S. monetary policy by setting the target for the federal funds rate. (source: https://www.federalreserve.gov/aboutthefed/fedexplained/monetary-policy.htm)
- The FOMC has 12 members: the 7 members of the Board of Governors, the president of the New York Fed and 4 of the other 11 Reserve Bank presidents on a rotating basis. (source: https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomc.htm)
- The FOMC holds 8 regularly scheduled meetings per year. (source: https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomc.htm)
- On September 16, 2026 the FOMC announced a 25 basis point increase in the federal funds target range to 3.75 to 4 percent, effective September 17, 2026. (source: https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm)
- At its October 28, 2026 meeting the FOMC held the target range at 3.75 to 4 percent. (source: https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261028a.htm)
- In March 2020 the FOMC cut the target range to 0 to 0.25 percent; by July 2023 it had raised it to 5.25 to 5.5 percent. (source: https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/openmarket.htm)
- Lenders use your credit score to help set the interest rate you are offered. (source: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-credit-score-en-315/)
