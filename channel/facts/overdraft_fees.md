# Overdraft fees

- An overdraft happens when a checking account does not have enough money to cover a transaction but the bank pays it anyway, usually charging a fee on top of the amount you must pay back. (source: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-finds-small-debit-purchases-lead-to-expensive-overdraft-charges/)
- The FDIC says overdraft fees vary by bank but may cost around $35 per transaction, and some banks also charge a fee for every day the account stays overdrawn. (source: https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-resource-center/2021-12/overdraft-and-account-fees)
- For debit card and ATM transactions, a bank can charge an overdraft fee only if the customer opted in; without opting in there is no fee, though the purchase may be declined. (source: https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-resource-center/2021-12/overdraft-and-account-fees)
- A CFPB study found most debit card overdraft fees were charged on purchases of $24 or less, and more than half of people who overdrew were back to a positive balance within three days. (source: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-finds-small-debit-purchases-lead-to-expensive-overdraft-charges/)
- In that study, borrowing $24 for 3 days and paying the median $34 overdraft fee worked out like a loan with a 17,000 percent APR. (source: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-finds-small-debit-purchases-lead-to-expensive-overdraft-charges/)
- The same study found opted-in accounts paid almost $260 a year in overdraft and NSF (bounced payment) fees, compared with just over $35 for accounts that had not opted in. (source: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-finds-small-debit-purchases-lead-to-expensive-overdraft-charges/)
- Banks with more than $1 billion in assets reported $11.96 billion in overdraft and NSF (bounced payment) fee revenue in 2019 and $5.83 billion in 2023, a drop of 51 percent. (source: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/data-spotlight-overdraft-nsf-revenue-in-2023-down-more-than-50-versus-pre-pandemic-levels-saving-consumers-over-6-billion-annually/)
- Linking a savings account to checking lets the bank pull money from savings to cover a shortfall; the transfer fee is typically less than an overdraft charge. (source: https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-resource-center/2021-12/overdraft-and-account-fees)
