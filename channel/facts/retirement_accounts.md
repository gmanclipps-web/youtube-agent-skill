# Retirement accounts

- A 401(k) lets an employee have part of each paycheck put into a retirement account at work; in a traditional 401(k) that money is generally not taxed when it goes in, and it and its investment gains are not taxed until withdrawn. (source: https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-sponsor/401k-plan-overview)
- Employers can add matching contributions, for example 50 cents for each dollar an employee puts in. (source: https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-contributions)
- The basic yearly 401(k) contribution limit was $22,500 in 2023, $23,000 in 2024 and $23,500 in 2025. (source: https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-contributions)
- With a traditional IRA, contributions may be tax-deductible and the money, including earnings, is generally not taxed until it is withdrawn. (source: https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/traditional-iras)
- With a Roth IRA, contributions are not deductible, but qualified withdrawals are tax-free, and the money can stay in the account as long as the owner lives. (source: https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/roth-iras)
- Withdrawals from an IRA or retirement plan before age 59 and a half generally owe an extra 10 percent tax unless an exception applies. (source: https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-tax-on-early-distributions)
- Starting in 2027, the federal Saver's Match will match up to 50 percent of what eligible low- and moderate-income savers put into a retirement plan or IRA, up to $1,000 per person each year. (source: https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/savers-match)
- IRS example: save $20 a month in 2027 ($240 total) with the full 50 percent match, and the government adds $120, for $360 saved in the first year. (source: https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/savers-match)
