# Index funds

- An index fund follows a passive strategy designed to get about the same return as a particular index, before fees. (source: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/mutual-funds-and-exchange-traded-1)
- An index fund invests mainly in the securities of the companies included in its chosen index. (source: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/mutual-funds-and-exchange-traded-1)
- Passive management usually means less trading, lower realized capital gains and lower fees than actively managed funds. (source: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/mutual-funds-and-exchange-traded-1)
- A mutual fund pools money from many investors; each share is part ownership of the fund's portfolio. (source: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/mutual-funds-and-exchange-traded-1)
- Funds can spread money across many companies and industries, which helps lower the risk if one company fails. (source: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/mutual-funds-and-exchange-traded-1)
- Even small differences in fees can mean large differences in returns over time. (source: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/mutual-funds-and-exchange-traded-1)
