# Compound interest

- Compound interest is the interest you earn on interest. (source: https://www.investor.gov/additional-resources/information/youth/teachers-classroom-resources/what-compound-interest)
- $100 earning 5 percent a year becomes $105 after the first year and $110.25 after the second: the extra 0.25 is interest earned on the $5 of interest. (source: https://www.investor.gov/additional-resources/information/youth/teachers-classroom-resources/what-compound-interest)
- Without adding another dime, that $100 at 5 percent grows to more than $162 in 10 years and almost $340 in 25 years. (source: https://www.investor.gov/additional-resources/information/youth/teachers-classroom-resources/what-compound-interest)
- Rule of 72: divide 72 by the interest rate to estimate how many years it takes money to double; at 9 percent it doubles about every 8 years. (source: https://www.investor.gov/additional-resources/information/youth/teachers-classroom-resources/what-compound-interest)
- Investor.gov example: skip a $2 pizza slice every day for a year and you save $730; invested at 5 percent it becomes $931.69 after 5 years and $3,155.02 after 30 years. (source: https://www.investor.gov/additional-resources/information/youth/teachers-classroom-resources/what-compound-interest)
- How often interest is compounded (annually, semiannually, quarterly, monthly or daily) changes how much money grows. (source: https://www.investor.gov/financial-tools-calculators/calculators/compound-interest-calculator)
