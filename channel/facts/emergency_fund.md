# Emergency fund

- An emergency fund is a cash reserve set aside for unplanned expenses or financial emergencies, such as car repairs, medical bills or a loss of income. (source: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/an-essential-guide-to-building-an-emergency-fund/)
- How much you need depends on your situation; looking at the unexpected expenses you have had before can help you set a goal. (source: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/an-essential-guide-to-building-an-emergency-fund/)
- Without savings, a shock can turn into debt from credit cards or loans, which can make a one-time expense grow because of interest and fees. (source: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/an-essential-guide-to-building-an-emergency-fund/)
- Saving all or part of a one-time payment, like a tax refund, can help set up an emergency fund quickly. (source: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/an-essential-guide-to-building-an-emergency-fund/)
- A bank or credit union account is generally considered one of the safest places to keep it. (source: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/an-essential-guide-to-building-an-emergency-fund/)
- Deposits are automatically insured to at least $250,000 at each FDIC-insured bank. (source: https://www.fdic.gov/resources/deposit-insurance)
